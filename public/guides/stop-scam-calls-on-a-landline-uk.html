How to Stop Scam Calls on a Landline (UK)

Landlines are still a common target for scam calls. Here's what's actually available from UK providers, and what each option does and doesn't cover.

Landline numbers tend to be older, more publicly listed, and more likely to belong to an older person — all of which makes them a disproportionate target for scam calls. The good news is there are several genuinely useful options, most of them free or low-cost.

Provider-level call blocking

Start with whichever company provides the landline service:

  • BT — offers BT Call Protect, a free service that screens incoming calls against a regularly updated list of known nuisance and scam numbers, blocking them before the phone rings.
  • Sky — offers similar nuisance call-blocking features; some setups may need "Call Divert" added to the line first, which can carry a small additional monthly cost from Sky (typically around £2.50/month) separate from any blocking feature itself.
  • Virgin Media — also offers call-screening options, again sometimes requiring Call Divert to be added first, with a similar potential small additional cost.
  • Other providers (TalkTalk, EE, Vodafone landline, etc.) — worth a direct call or a look at their website's nuisance-calls page, as most now offer something.

A single phone call to the provider asking "what nuisance and scam call blocking do you offer, and can you switch it on?" is often the single most effective step.

Register with the Telephone Preference Service

The TPS is the UK's free official register for opting out of marketing calls (0345 070 0707 or online). It reduces legitimate marketing calls within a few weeks, since companies must check against it every 28 days by law — but it has no effect on scam calls, which ignore the law entirely.

Dedicated call-blocking devices

Standalone call-blocking devices connect between the phone and the wall socket (or sit inline with a cordless phone base) and check every incoming number against a maintained database of known nuisance and scam numbers, often blocking silently or requiring the caller to announce themselves before the phone rings. These are a solid option for someone who relies mainly or entirely on a landline.

Ofcom's network-level changes

Since January 2025, Ofcom's updated guidance requires UK phone providers to identify and block calls arriving from abroad that fraudulently present a UK landline number — a technique scammers use to make an international call look like it's coming from a trusted local number. Some providers reported blocking around a million such calls a day within the first month of enforcement. This happens automatically at the network level and needs no action.

The gap all of the above share

Every option above works by matching against a list — a list of marketing companies (TPS), a list of known bad numbers (call blocking), or a list of spoofing patterns (Ofcom's rules). None of them can catch a scam call from a genuinely new number that hasn't been reported yet, which is common since scammers frequently rotate numbers.
