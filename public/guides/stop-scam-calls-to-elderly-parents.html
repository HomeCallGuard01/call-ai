How to Stop Scam Calls to Elderly Parents

Beyond general advice — the specific, practical options that actually reduce how many scam calls reach the phone.

If you're looking for concrete steps rather than general awareness advice, here's what actually works, roughly in order of effort required.

1. Register with the Telephone Preference Service (free)

The Telephone Preference Service (TPS) is the UK's official register for opting out of marketing calls. It's free, takes a few minutes online or by calling 0345 070 0707, and legitimate companies are legally required to check against it every 28 days.

Its limit: TPS only covers marketing calls from real UK companies following the law. It does nothing to stop scam calls, since scammers ignore the register entirely. Treat it as one layer, not the solution.

2. Use their phone provider's call-blocking features

Most UK landline and mobile providers now offer some level of nuisance-call blocking, often included free:

  • BT customers can use BT Call Protect, which intercepts known nuisance numbers before the phone rings.
  • Sky and Virgin Media landline customers have similar call-screening features, sometimes requiring "Call Divert" to be added to the line first — occasionally for a small extra monthly cost.
  • Mobile networks increasingly block calls that spoof a UK number from abroad, following updated Ofcom industry guidance that came into force in January 2025.

It's worth a five-minute call to their provider to ask what's available and switch it on — this is often the highest-value, lowest-effort step.

3. Consider a dedicated call-blocking device

For landlines specifically, dedicated call-blocking devices sit between the phone and the wall socket and screen incoming calls against a database of known scam numbers, sometimes requiring the caller to state their name before ringing through. These can meaningfully cut nuisance and scam calls for someone who relies mainly on a landline.

4. Add live monitoring for the calls that still get through

No blocklist can catch every scam number — scammers regularly use new or spoofed numbers that have never been reported before. This is the gap that call-blocking alone can't close: it can only block numbers already known to be bad.

5. Agree a family rule for what to do if a call gets through anyway

Even with every layer above in place, agree in advance: no money moves, no card or account details are given, and no one is let into the house, based on a phone call alone — always hang up and call back on a number you already have, not one the caller gives you.
