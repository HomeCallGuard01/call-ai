Can You Block a Specific Scam Number?

Short answer: yes, usually — but it solves less of the problem than most people expect.

If you've just had a scam call and want to stop that exact number reaching you again, here's what's actually possible.

On a mobile phone

Both iPhone and Android let you block a specific number directly from the recent calls list — usually by tapping the "i" or three-dot menu next to the call and choosing "Block this Caller" or similar. This stops that specific number calling or texting you again.

On a landline

This depends on the provider and equipment:

  • Many modern landline phones have a "block last caller" or "call reject" button or menu option built in.
  • Providers like BT offer call-blocking services (such as BT Call Protect) where specific numbers can be added to a personal blocklist, in addition to their automatic nuisance-call database.
  • Dedicated call-blocking devices, which sit between the phone and the wall socket, typically let you add numbers to a permanent block list as well as checking against their own maintained database.

Why blocking a specific number only ever helps a little

This is the important limitation to understand: scammers very rarely call from the same number twice. Numbers are cheap, easy to acquire, and frequently spoofed to look like something else entirely (see our guide to caller ID spoofing). Blocking the exact number that just called stops that one number — it does nothing about the next call from a different one, which is usually what happens.

This is true of every list-based approach, including the Telephone Preference Service and general nuisance-call blocklists: they're most effective against organisations that keep using the same numbers, and least effective against scammers who deliberately don't.

What actually helps beyond blocking individual numbers

  • Provider-level nuisance-call blocking, which screens against much larger, constantly updated databases rather than a personal list.
  • Network-level anti-spoofing measures, which Ofcom now requires UK providers to implement for calls from abroad impersonating UK numbers.
  • Monitoring what's actually said during a call, rather than only the number it came from — since this catches scam calls regardless of whether the number has ever been seen before.
