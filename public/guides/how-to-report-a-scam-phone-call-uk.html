How to Report a Scam Phone Call in the UK

Which number to use depends on what happened on the call. Here's exactly which route applies.

The UK has several official reporting routes for scam calls, and which one to use depends on whether money or details were shared, and whether the call claimed to be from your bank.

If a call claimed to be your bank, right now

Dial 159

159 is the UK's dedicated bank fraud hotline, run by Stop Scams UK. It connects you directly to your own bank's fraud team and works with over 99% of UK retail bank accounts (Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, NatWest, Nationwide, Santander, and most others). Unlike an ordinary phone number, 159 cannot be spoofed by a scammer, so it's the safest way to check whether a call was genuine.

If you've lost money or shared personal/financial details

Contact your bank immediately (159 is the quickest route), then report it to Action Fraud:

  • Online, 24/7, at actionfraud.police.uk
  • By phone on 0300 123 2040 (Monday–Friday, 8am–8pm) — England, Wales and Northern Ireland
  • In Scotland, report to Police Scotland by calling 101 instead

You'll be given a crime reference number, which your bank may need for its own investigation.

If you received a suspicious call but nothing was shared

You can still report the number, free of charge, without needing to describe what happened in detail:

Text 7726

Text the word "Call" followed by the scam number to 7726 (spells "SPAM" on a phone keypad). This reports the number to your mobile network, helping them identify and block it for other customers too. It's free and takes seconds.

If it was a suspicious text message rather than a call

The same 7726 short code accepts suspicious text messages too — forward the text itself to 7726 rather than typing "Call".

If it involved a fake email or website

The National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) has a dedicated reporting address for suspicious emails: forward them to report@phishing.gov.uk.

Why reporting matters even without a loss

Every report to 7726 or Action Fraud feeds into the data used to identify and block numbers and patterns at a network level — the same data behind Ofcom's requirement that providers block spoofed calls from abroad. Reporting a call that did no harm to you can still help stop it reaching someone else.
