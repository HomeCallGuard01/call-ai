How to Protect an Elderly Person's Landline

Setting up protection for someone else's phone is a different task to setting it up for your own — here's how to do it well, including what you can arrange without being there.

If you're setting up phone protection for a parent or relative rather than yourself, there are a few extra things worth thinking about: what needs a visit, what can be done over the phone, and how to do it in a way that respects their independence rather than undermining it.

What can be arranged remotely

Quite a lot can be set up without visiting in person:

  • Registering their number with the Telephone Preference Service — this can be done online on their behalf, or by phone with them present to confirm.
  • Calling their landline provider to ask about nuisance-call blocking features — often just needs the account holder's permission on the call, or a three-way call with them.
  • Setting up a monitoring service like Home Call Guard — signup and configuration happens through a web dashboard, and the only thing that needs doing on their end is a short one-time setup step on the phone itself (see below).

What usually needs a visit

  • Installing a physical call-blocking device — these plug in between the phone and the wall socket, so someone needs to be there to fit it (or walk them through it carefully on a call).
  • The one-time phone setup step most call-screening services need — typically dialling a short code once to route calls through the service. This is quick, but easier in person the first time, particularly if the phone is unfamiliar or has poor eyesight-friendly buttons.
  • Checking they understand and are comfortable with what's changed — always worth doing face to face rather than assuming a phone call has landed the message.

Respecting independence while setting this up

It's worth thinking about how any of this lands from their side. Being told "I've decided you need call protection" can feel like being told you can no longer be trusted to answer your own phone — even when the intention is entirely caring. A few things that help:

  • Frame it as something you're doing for peace of mind for yourself, not because you think they can't cope — this is often true anyway.
  • Explain clearly that trusted family and friends will never be affected — calls from people they know will always ring through exactly as before.
  • Involve them in the decision rather than presenting it as already done, where possible.
